## 1. How can gen-AI be used to improve the recruitment and retention of PGTAs in the department of Computer Science?

PGTA: Postgraduate teaching assistant

### Caveats

This project would depend on ethics approval to use the data and as it relates to humans and so is sensitive. It is not
guaranteed that approval would be given so this project may not be possible in the way it is currently outlined.

There is no funding for this project and no access to high performance computing. Any solution would be limited to use
of free tools and infrastructure.

### Problem

The department of computer science recruits more PGTAs than any other UCL department. The statistics from the 2024/25
recruitment cycle are:

- 166 modules that require PGTA support. This increases slightly each year.
- The skills required vary widely by module.
- 434 individual roles (each module has one or more roles). This equates to 22,000 hours requested.
- 800+ applications from 405 potential TAs received and processed.
- 267 individuals hired.
- ~300 PhD students in the department (figure cannot be verified).
- Each PhD student is asked to contribute 40 hours. To meet demand each would need to contribute 67 hours.
- Around half of the department's PhD students contribute to PGTA work. Recruitment is therefore open to external
  applicants, PhD
  students from other departments and in some instances MSc students. The undergraduate intern scheme fulfils a subset
  of specific roles.
- UCL caps the total PGTA hours per person to 180 per academic year; or no more than 6 hours average per week.

An undergraduate project in 2023/24 modelled the demand for PGTAs and produced a dashboard app and AI models of PGTA
demand. This may be relevant to consider and extend.

The scale of the recruitment gives rise to some challenges:

- lack of time for academics to scheduled and carry out interviews
- predicting which roles may be best suited to any given person based on their skills and experiences
- identifying which PGTAs are likely to return and/or contribute to more than one module
- understanding factors that might encourage more PhD students to carry out PGTA work
- understanding why some students do not wish to take on PGTA roles
- identifying training needs
- improving the attractiveness of roles based on limited job descriptions - could we enhance the 'adverts'?
- recruitment involves individuals - if AI is used, how do we ensure it is done so ethically and considering any
  potential bias?

There are likely opportunities in many of these to make improvements using gen AI tools. However, to identify these
opportunities would take research.

### Project opportunity

- Initial research into how AI is being used in recruitment and retention of staff. If available, research how this
  is more specifically being used to recruit and manage teaching assistants in universities.
- Identify potential aspects of the CS PGTA process/cycle that could be enhanced using AI.
- Research these aspects to identify how gen AI is being used, or how it may potentially be used in the future.
- Recommend the aspects that would most benefit from use of AI
- Design and develop a prototype solution based on your recommendations

## 2. Ethical AI and Bias Mitigation in Software Engineering education

AI tools are constantly evolving and can be used in almost all aspects of software engineering education:

- expert tutor to explain concepts
- writing and debugging code
- creating and evaluating designs
- etc

This raises interesting considerations in relation to education such as:

- Do students know how to use such tools?
- Can students recognise ethical considerations in the outputs of such tools?
- Should students use such tools?
- Does it improve or hinder the students' learning and ability to understand and apply techniques if they rely on AI?
- If a student chooses not to use AI tools, do assessment practices consider this?
- ... and many more

If we assume that AI capability and availability will continue to increase, then how can we adapt the teaching of
software engineering such that students understand ethical implications and can take relevant steps in their work to
mitigate potential concerns?

### Project opportunity

- Literature Review: Conduct a review of existing research on ethical AI, bias in AI systems, and fairness auditing
  techniques.
- Bias Identification: Investigate common sources of bias in AI models used in software engineering, such as biased
  training data, algorithmic bias, and bias in model evaluation metrics.
- Review student AI use: Consider how students are using AI in COMP0035 and COMP0034; and possibly their views on the ethical considerations.
- Ethical Guidelines/Education for Students:

    - What do we need to teach students so that they use AI ethically in their learning and also in the work they
      produce (data science applications)?
    - Propose ethical guidelines and best practices for using, developing and
      deploying AI models in software engineering to ensure fairness and accountability.
    - Consider developing a prototype
      that helps students develop code with practical examples that illustrate how bias and fairness can be achieved.
